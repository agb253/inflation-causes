Summary Notes
Estimated Budgetary Effects of Public Law 117-169,
to Provide for Reconciliation Pursuant to Title II of S. Con. Res. 14

CBO estimates that titles I, II, III, IV, VI, VII, and VIII of P.L. 117-169 will not increase on-budget deficits after 2031.
The revenues and outlays of the Social Security trust funds and the net cash flow of the Postal Service are classified as off-budget.
CBO cannot determine whether title V, part 6, of P.L. 117-169 will increase or decrease deficits after 2031 because of significant uncertainty concerning the scope of federal
mineral leasing and energy development activities and related receipts after 2031. CBO estimates that the remaining provisions of title V will not increase deficits after 2031.


a.    The Budget Control Act of 2011 requires the annual sequestration of nonexempt mandatory spending programs. The Office of Management and Budget determines which
      accounts are subject to reductions under that law. Some of the accounts affected by titles I and II of P.L. 117-169 are subject to sequestration; this estimate reflects the
      effects of sequestration on those accounts.
b.    Postal Service cash flows are recorded in the Postal Service Fund in the federal budget and are classified as off-budget. Title VII of P.L. 117-169 directs the transfer of funds
      from the Treasury to the Postal Service Fund; that transfer will be classified as an on-budget transaction.
c.    CBO expects that the provisions in title I of P.L. 117-169 that increase funding for tax enforcement activities also will increase revenues. However, under guidelines agreed to
      by the legislative and executive branches, that change is not included in this cost estimate, although it will be reflected in CBO’s future baseline budget projections. (For more
      information on those guidelines, see Congressional Budget Office, CBO Explains Budgetary Scorekeeping Guidelines, January 2021, www.cbo.gov/publication/56507.) CBO
      estimates that as a result of those increases in outlays, revenues will increase by $180 billion over the 2022-2031 period.
